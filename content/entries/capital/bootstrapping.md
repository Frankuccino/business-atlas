Bootstrapping means growing from revenue. Venture capital means selling equity to buy growth faster than revenue allows. They are not better or worse; they are different instruments with different terms.

The real trade-off is not money. It is control and obligation. Venture money requires a return, which requires an exit, which constrains every later decision. Bootstrapping leaves the founders free and caps the speed.

## Worked example

A company needs $2,000,000 to reach its next stage:

    Bootstrapped path:
      Current revenue          $400,000/yr
      Contribution margin      70%  →  $280,000 available
      Time to raise $2,000,000  ≈ 7 years of retained profit
      Equity given up           0%

    Venture path:
      Raise $2,000,000         now
      Equity given up           20-25%
      Obligation                a return, which requires an exit in ~7 years
      Growth rate               constrained by the market, not by cash

If the market is large and the window is short, the venture path is the only one that can win it. If the market is modest and the business is profitable from month one, the venture path gives away control for speed the business does not need.

## Watch for

- **Venture money is a means, not a milestone.** Raising is not success; it is a loan against a future you now have to build.
- **Runway is the real cost.** See [runway](#runway). Venture funding extends it, which is valuable only if you use the time to become fundable again on better terms.
- **Bootstrapping has a ceiling.** Some markets cannot be served at a scale that supports the team, and no amount of patience changes that.
