The capitalisation table lists every holder of equity and their share. It starts simple — two founders, 50/50 — and grows complicated with every grant, every round, and every early agreement made on a handshake.

The reason it matters is that the table determines control. A founder who has sold 60% across rounds may still run the company day to day, but no longer decides its fate.

## Worked example

    Holder                    Shares     Percentage
    Founder A                  600,000       54.5%
    Founder B                  300,000       27.3%
    Option pool               150,000       13.6%
    Angel investor              50,000        4.5%
    Total                    1,100,000      100.0%

Now a seed round issues 200,000 new shares to an investor:

    Total shares             1,300,000
    Founder A                  600,000  →  46.2%
    Investor                   200,000  →  15.4%

Founder A's stake fell from 54.5% to 46.2% without selling anything. That is dilution, and it is why the size of the option pool and the valuation of each round are worth more argument than they usually receive.

## Watch for

- **Handshake equity is a cap-table problem waiting.** A co-founder who leaves at month three with unvested shares still holds them. Vest everything.
- **The option pool dilutes you, not the investor.** It is created before the round and paid for by existing holders, which founders often discover only after signing.
- **Model the next two rounds before signing this one.** The terms that look fine now can leave you without control by the round after.
