Dilution is what happens to your percentage when new shares are issued. It is not the same as losing money — your share of a much larger company can be worth far more — but it is permanent and it compounds.

The distinction founders miss is between percentage and value. Giving away 20% feels like losing a fifth of the company. If the capital turns a $5,000,000 company into a $25,000,000 one, the founder's 80% of $25,000,000 is worth far more than 100% of $5,000,000.

## Worked example

A founder holds 1,000,000 of 1,000,000 shares — 100%.

    Round 1: 250,000 new shares
      Total 1,250,000 → founder holds 80.0%

    Round 2: 500,000 new shares
      Total 1,750,000 → founder holds 57.1%

    Round 3: 500,000 new shares
      Total 2,250,000 → founder holds 44.4%

Three rounds took the stake from 100% to 44.4%. If the company is now worth $40,000,000, that stake is $17,760,000 — against $5,000,000 for the whole company at the start. The percentage fell by more than half and the value rose more than three times.

## Watch for

- **Dilution is decided before the round, not during.** The option pool and the valuation are set at term-sheet stage.
- **Model to the exit, not to the next round.** A stake that looks thin at round three may still be the largest single holding.
- **Founders dilute twice.** Once through investment and once through hiring — the option pool comes out of existing holders' shares.
