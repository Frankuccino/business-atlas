Fundraising is a process with a long cycle and a binary outcome, and the two together are what make it dangerous. A company that starts raising with six months of runway will be out of money before the process finishes.

The compression point matters most. Investors compare you against their other deals, and a round that is open for a year looks like a round nobody wanted. Running a focused window creates the competition that produces a term sheet.

## Worked example

    Month 1-2   target list, materials, warm intros
    Month 3     30 first meetings in 3 weeks
    Month 4     second meetings, 3-4 serious conversations
    Month 5     term sheets, negotiation
    Month 6     diligence, legal, close

Starting this with four months of runway means the company is out of cash at month 4 — before the term sheet. The fix is not raising faster; it is starting earlier, which is why [runway](#runway) is the number that governs the whole process.

## Watch for

- **A term sheet is not money.** Diligence and legal can take six weeks, and a term sheet can be withdrawn. Do not stop raising until the funds clear.
- **Fundraising consumes the founders.** The people who should be running the business are pitching for a quarter. Plan the operating plan around that.
- **Momentum is real.** A round that closes quickly attracts more interest; one that drags loses it. Compress the window deliberately.
