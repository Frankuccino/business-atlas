Raising money is not a milestone. It is a trade: you give up a permanent share
of a business that may be worth something later, in exchange for time and
resources now. The trade is worth making only when the time and resources buy
something revenue cannot.

## Before you start

Answer the four questions. If the answer to "what will you do with it that you
cannot do with revenue" is "grow faster," you have not answered the question.
Faster is not a use of funds; it is an outcome. The use of funds is specific:
hire three engineers, open a second market, fund a twelve-month sales cycle.

## The process

Fundraising is a sales process with a single buyer at a time. Treat it like
one. Run a tight timeline, create competition between investors, and never let
one investor's pace set your company's pace.

## The terms

Valuation is the number people remember. The terms are the numbers that matter.
Liquidation preference, participation, anti-dilution, and board composition
decide who gets paid and who decides. Read the term sheet twice, and have a
lawyer read it once more.

## The cost

The real cost of a round is not the dilution. It is the reporting, the board
meetings, the investor updates, and the loss of the ability to change your mind
quietly. Count that cost before you take the money.
