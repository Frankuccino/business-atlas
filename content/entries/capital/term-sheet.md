A term sheet is the agreement on price and terms before the legal documents. It is short, and the important clauses are the ones that decide what happens when things go badly.

Valuation gets the attention. But the clauses that matter most are control (board seats, protective provisions), liquidation preference (who gets paid first and how much), and option pool size.

## Worked example

Two offers for the same company:

    Offer A:  $3,000,000 at a $12,000,000 pre-money valuation
              1x non-participating preference, 2 board seats (1 investor)

    Offer B:  $3,000,000 at a $10,000,000 pre-money valuation
              2x participating preference, 3 board seats (2 investor), full ratchet

Offer A is better despite the higher valuation of Offer B being... no — Offer A has the higher valuation and cleaner terms. Offer B takes more control, guarantees the investor double their money before anyone else sees a return, and adjusts the price downward if a later round is raised lower. At a modest exit, Offer B can return more to the investor than the founders receive.

## Watch for

- **Liquidation preference changes everything at a low exit.** A 2x participating preference can take most of a small exit before founders see anything.
- **Protective provisions are control.** A veto over ordinary decisions is more restrictive than a board seat.
- **Read the anti-dilution clause.** A full ratchet punishes a down round far more severely than a weighted average, and it is the clause that destroys founder value.
