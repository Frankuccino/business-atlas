The balance sheet is a photograph, not a film. It lists assets (what the business owns), liabilities (what it owes), and equity (what belongs to the owners) at one instant, and the two sides always balance.

Its value is in the structure. Current assets against current liabilities tells you whether the business can pay its bills in the next year. Debt against equity tells you how much of the company is borrowed and how much is owned.

## Worked example

    Assets
      Cash                    200,000
      Accounts receivable     150,000
      Inventory               100,000
      Equipment               250,000
      Total                   700,000

    Liabilities
      Accounts payable        120,000
      Loan                    180,000
      Total                   300,000

    Equity                  =   400,000
    Total                   =   700,000

Current ratio = (200,000 + 150,000 + 100,000) / 120,000 = 3.75. The business can cover its short-term obligations comfortably. But equity of $400,000 against $180,000 of debt means it is financed mostly by its owners, which is conservative and slow.

## Watch for

- **It is a moment, not a trend.** A healthy balance sheet the day before a bad quarter is still a healthy balance sheet. Read it over time.
- **Inventory and receivables are not cash.** They are assets that must convert. A large receivables balance can hide a cash problem.
- **Off-balance-sheet items.** Leases, guarantees, and commitments may not appear as liabilities and still bind the business.
