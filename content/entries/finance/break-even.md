Break-even is where total revenue equals total cost. It is the smallest volume at which the business stops losing money on its own operations.

It matters because it separates "we need more customers" from "the model is wrong." A company far below break-even can fix itself with growth. A company that cannot reach break-even at any realistic volume has a pricing or cost problem, and no amount of marketing fixes that.

## Worked example

A coffee cart has fixed costs of $3,000 a month. Each coffee sells for $4 and costs $1 to make:

    Contribution margin per unit = 4 - 1 = 3
    Break-even = 3,000 / 3 = 1,000 coffees a month

That is about 33 coffees a day. If the cart sells 600 a month it loses $1,200; at 1,500 it makes $1,500. The break-even number is the line between those two futures.

Now raise the price to $5 with the same cost: contribution margin becomes $4 and break-even falls to 750 coffees. The same cart, the same costs, a 25% smaller target.

## Watch for

- **Fixed costs move.** Rent, salaries, and tooling are decisions. Break-even moves when they do.
- **Contribution margin, not gross margin.** Forgetting variable selling costs makes break-even look closer than it is.
- **Break-even is not cash flow.** You can be above break-even on paper and still run out of cash if customers pay slowly.
