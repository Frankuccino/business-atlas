Burn rate is net cash outflow per month: what you spent minus what you earned. A company with $300,000 of costs and $100,000 of revenue has a burn rate of $200,000.

The number that matters is not the burn itself but the ratio of cash to burn — that is [runway](#runway). Burn tells you the speed; runway tells you the time.

## Worked example

A startup has $1,200,000 in the bank.

- Month 1-3: costs $250,000, revenue $50,000 → burn $200,000/month
- Month 4-6: costs $280,000, revenue $80,000 → burn $200,000/month
- Month 7: hires two engineers, costs $340,000, revenue $90,000 → burn $250,000/month

    Average burn over 7 months = (200k*6 + 250k) / 7 = 207,143

At $207,000 a month, $1,200,000 lasts about 5.8 months. If the next fundraise takes four months, the company is fine. If it takes eight, it is not — and the decision to hire was made in month 7 without knowing that.

## Watch for

- **Gross vs net burn.** Gross burn is total spend; net burn subtracts revenue. Investors ask for both because they behave very differently.
- **Burn is a lagging indicator.** By the time the number is bad, the decisions that caused it are months old.
- **See [runway](#runway)** — the two are useless apart.
