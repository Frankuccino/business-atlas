A price change is not a number change. It is a claim about who your product is
for and what it is worth. Most teams treat it as a spreadsheet exercise and are
surprised when the spreadsheet is right and the business breaks anyway.

## Before you change anything

Answer the four questions in the trigger. If you cannot answer "what is the
value to the customer" in one sentence, stop. You are not ready to price; you
are ready to learn.

## The test

Test the new price on new customers first. Existing customers carry switching
costs and goodwill that distort the signal. A price that works on new customers
and fails on old ones is a communication problem, not a pricing problem.

## The rollout

Grandfather existing customers for a fixed period, then migrate. Announce the
change before it takes effect, with the reason. Customers tolerate a price
increase they understand and resent one they discover on their invoice.

## The number that matters

Gross margin at the new price, at the same volume. If volume drops, the question
is whether the margin gain exceeds the revenue loss. That is arithmetic, and
it is the only part of this that is.
