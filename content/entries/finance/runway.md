Runway is cash divided by burn. It converts a spending rate into a deadline, and it is the single number that should be on the founder's dashboard.

It is also the number most often misstated. Founders quote runway on gross burn while ignoring revenue, or on a burn rate that has already changed, or on cash that is committed to something else.

## Worked example

- Cash on hand: $900,000
- Monthly burn (net): $150,000

    Runway = 900,000 / 150,000 = 6 months

A fundraise typically takes four to six months from first meeting to cash in the bank. With six months of runway, a founder who starts raising now is raising at the last possible moment, with no leverage and no alternatives. The correct move is to start at twelve months, when there is still a choice.

Now suppose revenue grows so net burn falls to $90,000:

    Runway = 900,000 / 90,000 = 10 months

The cash did not change. The deadline moved four months — which is the whole point of improving unit economics before raising.

## Watch for

- **Runway is not a plan.** It is a constraint. A plan that assumes more runway than exists is a hope.
- **Cash on hand is not always spendable.** Restricted cash, outstanding obligations, and minimum balances all reduce the real number.
- **See [bootstrapping](#bootstrapping)** — the alternative to raising is growing into your own runway.
