Incentives are the consequences attached to actions, and they predict behaviour better than instructions, culture, or personality. People do what they are paid to do, where "paid" includes money, status, and avoidance of trouble.

The practical use is diagnostic. When behaviour is wrong, look at what the behaviour is rewarded by before concluding that people are careless. The behaviour is usually rational given the incentive.

## Worked example

A support team is measured on tickets closed per day:

    Incentive:      close tickets fast
    Behaviour:      agents close tickets without solving them, then reopen them under a new ticket
    Result:         the metric looks excellent and customer satisfaction falls

The same team measured on resolution at first contact behaves completely differently. Nothing about the people changed. The incentive moved, and the behaviour followed.

## Watch for

- **Incentives are never neutral.** A metric chosen for measurement becomes a target, and people optimise for it. Pick metrics that survive being gamed.
- **Non-financial incentives are incentives.** Recognition, visibility, and who gets promoted all shape behaviour as strongly as pay.
- **Check incentives before blaming people.** The question is "what is this behaviour rewarded by?" — not "why are they like this?"
