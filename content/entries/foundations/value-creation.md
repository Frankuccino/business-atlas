Business has two halves. **Value creation** is making something worth more than the sum of its parts — a product that saves time, a service that removes risk, a market that connects two sides. **Value capture** is keeping a share of that surplus as profit.

The order matters. Creation comes first, and capture follows as a consequence. The reverse — designing a capture mechanism and hoping value appears — is how you get a business model that works only while no one is looking.

## Worked example

A logistics startup connects truckers with shippers:

- Without it: a shipper pays $2,000 and waits two days to find a truck.
- With it: the shipper pays $1,600 and gets a truck in two hours.

The value created is roughly $400 in price plus the time saved. If the startup keeps $120 as its fee, it created $400 and captured $120. The surplus is real, so both sides return.

Now suppose the startup instead charged $450 while providing nothing the shipper could not do alone. It captures more than it creates. The moment a cheaper connector appears, it is gone — because there was nothing holding the customer there.

## Watch for

- **Capture without creation is a toll, not a business.** Tolls last exactly as long as the road is the only route.
- **Creating without capturing is a hobby.** Generous surplus with no margin runs out of money.
