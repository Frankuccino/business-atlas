A cognitive bias is a predictable way the mind reaches a wrong conclusion from correct information. Confirmation bias, sunk cost, anchoring, and survivorship bias are all examples, and all of them feel like clear thinking from the inside.

The practical point is that biases are not fixed by awareness. Knowing about confirmation bias does not stop you seeking confirming evidence. What works is process: requiring the opposing case, pre-committing to a decision rule, and separating the person who proposes from the person who decides.

## Worked example

A founder must decide whether to launch a second product line:

    Confirmation bias:  reads three success stories, ignores the twenty failures.
    Sunk cost:           "we've already spent $80,000 on it" — that money is gone either way.
    Anchoring:           the first revenue estimate of $500,000 frames every later revision.
    Survivorship:        the one comparable company that succeeded is the only one anyone can name.

Countermeasures: seek out the failures deliberately; decide on future costs and future returns only; have someone independent produce the first estimate; ask what the base rate is for companies in this position.

## Watch for

- **Awareness is not a defence.** The bias you can name is still operating. Process is the defence.
- **Bias is strongest where stakes are high and feedback is slow.** Exactly the conditions of a strategic decision.
- **Look for the decision you made fastest.** Slow, argued decisions are more likely to have been examined; instant ones are more likely to be bias wearing the costume of intuition.
