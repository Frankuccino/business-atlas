A board meeting is a decision-making forum disguised as a reporting exercise.
Most founders treat it as the latter and are surprised when it produces no
decisions. The fix is to design the meeting around the decisions, not the data.

## Before the meeting

Send the deck a week ahead, with the narrative, not just the numbers. A board
that reads the deck in the meeting is a board that is not deciding. The meeting
should start with the assumption that everyone has read it, and spend its time on
the questions it raised.

## The agenda

Three items, in this order:

1. What changed since last time.
2. What decisions need to be made now.
3. What help you need from the board.

Item one is short. Item two is the meeting. Item three is why the board exists.

## The bad news

Put it first, not last. A board that hears bad news in the first five minutes
has time to help. A board that hears it in the last five minutes has time to
react. The reaction is not the same as the help.

## After the meeting

Send minutes within 48 hours, including the decisions and the owners. A meeting
that produces no written record produces no accountability, and a board that is
not accountable to its own decisions is a board that is performing.
