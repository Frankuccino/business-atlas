Decisions come in two kinds. A reversible decision can be undone at a cost smaller than the cost of deciding slowly. An irreversible one cannot, or only at a cost that changes the business.

The value of the distinction is speed. Most decisions in a young company are reversible, and treating them as permanent creates a culture where nothing happens without a meeting.

## Worked example

    Decision                          Class          Right process
    Change the pricing page copy      Reversible     Ship it today, measure for a week
    Hire a second engineer            Mostly         Decide in days; you can manage them out
    Sign a 3-year office lease        Irreversible   Diligence, alternatives, board sign-off
    Sunset a feature                  Reversible     Do it; re-add it if customers revolt
    Take venture money                Irreversible   Term sheet, references, long deliberation

The pricing change and the feature sunset are the same kind of decision. One is made in a day and the other in a quarter purely because of habit, and the difference is weeks of learning.

## Watch for

- **Reversibility is about cost, not about regret.** A bad hire can be undone; it is expensive, not irreversible.
- **Speed has a cost too.** A reversible decision made without anyone checking it can still be wrong — the answer is to measure, not to deliberate.
- **Irreversible decisions deserve dissent.** The goal is not consensus but the strongest argument against, stated before the decision is locked.
