A contract is a record of an agreement that a court can enforce. Its real value is not that it makes people behave — it is that it states, in advance, what happens when they do not.

The clauses that decide the outcome of a dispute are rarely the price. They are liability caps, termination rights, and who owns what if the relationship ends.

## Worked example

A development agency signs a client. Four clauses matter more than the fee:

    Liability cap      "Our total liability is limited to the fees paid."
                       Without it, a bug could expose the agency to the client's whole loss.

    Termination        "Either party may terminate on 30 days' notice."
                       Without it, the agency can be locked into an unhappy client for years.

    IP assignment      "Work product transfers on full payment."
                       Without it, the client owns nothing until they have paid — and vice versa.

    Payment terms      "Net 30, with a 1.5% monthly late fee."
                       Without it, the agency finances the client.

A contract with a generous fee and none of these clauses is worse than one with a lower fee and all of them.

## Watch for

- **The contract is the relationship.** If you cannot agree on the failure terms, the relationship is already in trouble.
- **Read the termination clause first.** It tells you how you get out, which is the question you will ask when things go wrong.
- **Do not sign under pressure.** A deadline imposed by the other side is a reason to read more carefully, not less.
