An entity type is the legal container the business operates inside. The common choices are sole proprietorship, LLC, and corporation, and they differ mainly in liability and taxation.

The two questions that decide it are: can a customer or creditor reach your personal assets, and does the business pay tax separately from you? Everything else is detail.

## Worked example

    Type             Liability         Tax              Can raise equity
    Sole trader      Personal assets   Through you      No
    LLC              Protected         Through you      Awkward
    C-corp           Protected         Business + you   Yes, cleanly

A freelance designer with no debt and no partners may be fine as a sole trader — simple, cheap, and the liability risk is low. A software company with outside investors must be a C-corp, because investors need shares and the liability protection. Choosing the sole trader form and then trying to raise money means converting later, which costs legal fees and delays the round.

## Watch for

- **Entity choice interacts with funding.** See [cap-table](#cap-table). Investors cannot easily buy into an LLC, and converting later is expensive.
- **The cheapest form is not the best form.** A sole trader pays less to set up and can be personally bankrupted by a contract dispute.
- **It varies by jurisdiction.** The names and rules differ by country and state. Get local advice; this entry describes the pattern, not your jurisdiction's law.
