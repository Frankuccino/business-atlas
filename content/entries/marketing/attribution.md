Attribution is the answer to "which marketing caused this sale?" It is a modelling problem, not a measurement problem, because the customer's path is not observable end to end.

Every model is a simplification. Last-click gives all credit to the final touch. First-click gives it all to the first. Linear splits it evenly. Each produces a different budget, and none is correct.

## Worked example

A customer sees a podcast mention, Googles the category a week later, clicks a paid ad, and buys. Three models:

    Last-click:   paid search 100%, podcast 0%
    First-click:  podcast 100%, paid search 0%
    Linear:       33% each

Under last-click the podcast looks worthless and gets cancelled. Under first-click the paid search gets cancelled — and the customer now has no ad to click. Both decisions are wrong, and both are made from the same underlying behaviour.

## Watch for

- **State your model.** An attribution number without its model is uninterpretable, and two teams using different models will disagree about the same data.
- **Incrementality is the honest test.** Hold out a channel or a region and measure what sales disappear. Everything else is inference.
- **Do not over-invest in precision.** A roughly right model applied consistently beats a precise model applied once.
