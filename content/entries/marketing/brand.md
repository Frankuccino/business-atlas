A brand is what a customer expects before they experience the product. It is accumulated, not declared: every support reply, every late shipment, every price change adds to it or subtracts from it.

Its commercial value is concrete. A trusted brand converts better at the same price, survives a mistake that would kill an unknown one, and can charge more because the risk of choosing it feels lower.

## Worked example

Two suppliers sell an identical component at $10. One is well known, the other unknown:

    Known brand:   converts 8% of enquiries, supports $12 price
    Unknown brand: converts 3%, supports $10 price

On 1,000 enquiries the known brand makes 80 sales at $12 = $960; the unknown makes 30 at $10 = $300. The product is identical. The brand is worth more than three times the revenue, and it was built from reliability over years, not from a redesign.

## Watch for

- **Brand is a liability when broken.** A promise the product does not keep turns the brand against you, because it raises expectations you then miss.
- **Branding is not the brand.** A new identity cannot create trust; it can only express trust that already exists.
- **Brand compounds slowly and erodes fast.** One bad quarter can undo two years of consistency, which is why it is an operational commitment, not a campaign.
