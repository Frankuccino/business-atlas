Channel economics is the discipline of comparing acquisition routes on cost *and* customer quality. Cost per acquisition alone is misleading, because a cheap channel can deliver customers who leave immediately.

The comparison that matters is CAC against the lifetime value that channel produces. A channel with high cost and high value beats a channel with low cost and low value.

## Worked example

    Channel        CAC     Avg LTV   LTV/CAC   Verdict
    Paid search    $300     $900      3.0x    keep
    Paid social    $180     $300      1.7x    fix or cut
    Content/SEO    $120   $1,200      10.0x   scale
    Referral        $80   $1,000      12.5x   scale hard

Paid social looks affordable at $180 but its customers are worth $300 — barely viable. Content looks expensive to produce but delivers the best customers at the lowest effective cost. Referral is the standout and usually the most neglected.

## Watch for

- **Attribution corrupts the comparison.** See [attribution](#attribution) — the channel credited with a sale is often not the channel that caused it.
- **Channels saturate.** Doubling spend on a working channel usually raises its cost. The curve matters, not the point.
- **Channel fit is strategic.** A channel that reaches your segment is worth more than a cheap one that does not.
