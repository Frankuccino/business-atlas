A funnel is a model of how strangers become customers: awareness, interest, consideration, purchase. Each stage loses people, and the point of the model is to see where the losses are.

Without it, marketing decisions are made on totals. "We need more customers" is not actionable. "Our trial-to-paid conversion is 4% against an industry 12%" is.

## Worked example

    Stage                     Count    Conversion
    Ad impressions            100,000      —
    Clicks                      2,000     2.0%
    Signups                       400      20%
    Activated                    160      40%
    Paid                          64      40%

The worst step is impressions to clicks at 2%. But the biggest absolute loss is signups to activated: 400 people sign up and 240 never reach the value. Fixing onboarding from 40% to 60% would take paid customers from 64 to 96 — a 50% increase in revenue from the same traffic.

## Watch for

- **Optimise the worst stage, not the biggest number.** Ten thousand more impressions means nothing if activation is broken.
- **Stages must be defined by behaviour, not by your org chart.** "Marketing-qualified lead" is a handoff, not a customer action.
- **A funnel hides the loop.** Customers who refer others create a second path in. See [channels](#channels) for the economics of each path.
