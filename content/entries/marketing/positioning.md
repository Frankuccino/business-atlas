Positioning is the deliberate choice of what you are and are not. It is not a slogan; it is a decision about which comparison you want to be judged in.

A clear position makes every other decision easier. Pricing, features, channels, and messaging all follow from it. A vague position makes every decision arbitrary, which is why undifferentiated products compete on price and always lose.

## Worked example

A project-management tool could position against "spreadsheets" or against "Jira."

- Against spreadsheets: for teams that outgrew them. Message: "your spreadsheet breaks at fifty rows." Buyer: a small team, price-sensitive, short sales cycle.
- Against Jira: for teams that find Jira too heavy. Message: "real work, not ticket queues." Buyer: an engineering team, higher budget, longer cycle.

Both describe the same software. They are different products to different buyers, at different prices, through different channels. Picking one is positioning; picking neither is competing on price against both.

## Watch for

- **Positioning is a sacrifice.** Saying you are for everyone means you are for no one. The excluded segment is the point.
- **It must be defensible.** A position the market does not believe is just marketing. See [competitive-advantage](#competitive-advantage).
- **Positioning precedes messaging.** Writing copy before deciding the position produces words that describe features instead of a reason to buy.
