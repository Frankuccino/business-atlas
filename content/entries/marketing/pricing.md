Pricing is the most under-used lever in business. Founders price from cost or from competitors; the better question is what the customer's alternative costs them.

Cost-plus pricing ignores the customer entirely. Competitor-based pricing hands over the decision about your value to someone else. Value-based pricing asks what the customer would otherwise pay — in money, time, or risk — and captures a share of that.

## Worked example

A tool saves a bookkeeper 10 hours a month. A bookkeeper costs $40 an hour, so the value is $400 a month:

    Value-based price:  $40–80/month  (captures 10–20% of value)
    Cost-plus price:    $15/month     (cost + margin)
    Competitor price:   $50/month     (whatever the rival charges)

The cost-plus price leaves $385 of value with the customer. That is not generosity; it is money left on the table that funds your competitor's growth. A price of $60 captures a fair share and still saves the customer $340.

## Watch for

- **Price is a signal.** A price far below the value signals low quality; a price far above it invites a competitor. The wrong signal costs more than the wrong number.
- **Anchoring matters.** The first number the customer sees shapes every later comparison. Discounting trains them to wait for discounts.
- **Raise prices on the existing base last.** Existing customers are the hardest to re-price and the most visible. Test on new customers first.
