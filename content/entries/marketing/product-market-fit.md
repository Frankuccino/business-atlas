Product-market fit is the point where a real group of customers wants what you have enough to keep paying for it. It is not the same as having users, or having revenue, or having a good product.

The distinction matters because the failure modes are different. Before fit, growth spending is wasted — you are filling a bucket with no bottom. After fit, growth spending compounds, because the customers who arrive stay.

## Worked example

The standard test asks users how they would feel without the product. In a survey of 200:

    Very disappointed   90   (45%)
    Somewhat disappointed 60   (30%)
    Not disappointed     50   (25%)

Above 40% "very disappointed" is the usual threshold for fit. Now suppose the same survey a quarter earlier:

    Very disappointed   55   (27%)

The product was close but not there. Spending on paid acquisition at 27% would have bought customers who left; at 45% the same spend compounds.

## Watch for

- **Fit is per segment.** A product can have fit with one group and none with another. Segment before concluding.
- **Fit decays.** A market changes and competitors appear. The survey is a snapshot, not a permanent state.
- **Fit is not traction.** A waiting list is interest, not fit. Fit requires that customers stay and pay once the novelty fades.
