Segmentation is splitting a market into groups that want different things. It is the step between "who is our customer?" and "what do we build for them?"

The trap is segmenting by description instead of by need. Two companies in the same industry and of the same size may need entirely different things. Segmenting by what they are rather than what they need produces groups that cannot be served differently.

## Worked example

A bookkeeping tool's market splits by need:

    Segment A — freelancers: want speed and low price, buy alone, churn fast
    Segment B — 10-person agencies: want client management, buy for a team, churn slowly
    Segment C — 100-person firms: want compliance and integrations, buy through procurement, churn never

Same software, three different products in practice. Segment B values a feature A would never pay for and C would find trivial. Pricing, messaging, and roadmap all differ. Serving all three with one message produces a product that is mediocre for each.

## Watch for

- **Segments must be actionable.** If you cannot reach the group differently, it is not a segment — it is a description.
- **Start with one.** Serving two segments well beats serving four badly. Expand from a segment you own.
- **Segments change as you grow.** The segment that fits a ten-person company may be wrong at a hundred. Revisit it.
