Anchoring is the cognitive bias where the first number in a discussion becomes the baseline for everything that follows. It operates even when the number is obviously arbitrary.

The commercial consequence is that the order of speaking matters. Whoever states a number first has set the range, and the other party's counter will be pulled toward it rather than toward the true value.

## Worked example

A supplier negotiation over an annual contract:

    Buyer speaks first:   "We were thinking $80,000."
    Supplier was prepared to accept $100,000. Final: $92,000.

    Supplier speaks first: "Our rate is $140,000."
    Buyer was prepared to pay $110,000. Final: $118,000.

The underlying value is the same in both cases. The first number shifted the settlement by roughly $20,000 in each direction — about 20% of the contract — with no change in the work.

## Watch for

- **Know your number before the meeting.** If you cannot state a well-supported figure, you will accept theirs.
- **A high anchor needs support.** An aggressive first offer with no reasoning behind it damages credibility. Anchor high and explain why.
- **Anchors work in both directions.** A salary candidate who names a range first shifts the offer; so does a customer who states a budget.
