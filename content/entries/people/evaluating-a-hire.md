Hiring is a decision to spend a large amount of money on a bet about a person.
The bet is worth making only when the person will do something specific that
creates more value than they cost, and when you can tell whether they are doing
it.

## The role

Before you look at candidates, write down what the person will produce in their
first 90 days. Not "lead the team" or "own the function" — those are titles.
Write the deliverables. If you cannot, the role is not real yet, and hiring
will not make it real.

## The candidate

A resume is a story the candidate tells about themselves. A work sample is
evidence. Prefer the second. Give them a real problem from your business, with
real constraints, and watch how they think. The interview tells you how they
talk; the sample tells you how they work.

## The reference

References given by the candidate are marketing. References you find yourself
are signal. Ask the reference what the candidate was like on their worst day, and
listen for the pause before the answer.

## The decision

Hire when the cost of not hiring is higher than the cost of hiring, and when you
have a specific answer to "what will change." A hire made to relieve pressure
relieves it briefly and creates a permanent cost. A hire made to create value
compounds.
