Hiring is a loop, not an event, because the alternative is a panic hire when someone leaves. A maintained pipeline turns a crisis into a decision.

The failure mode is hiring for activity instead of outcome. A job description listing tools and years produces candidates who can name the tools. Describing the result the role owns produces candidates who can describe producing it.

## Worked example

Two versions of the same role:

    Bad:   "We need a marketing manager. 3-5 years experience, HubSpot, SEO, paid social."
    Good:  "We need someone who can make our paid channel profitable. You will own CAC from $180 to under $120 in two quarters, and you will tell us what you learned either way."

The second description can be assessed. A candidate who asks "what is the current conversion rate and who is it best for?" is demonstrating the job. One who asks "what is the budget?" is demonstrating that they have not thought about the outcome.

## Watch for

- **Hire slowly, fire quickly.** The asymmetry is brutal: six months of a bad hire costs more than six months of an unfilled role.
- **The interview is a prediction, not a verdict.** Structured questions asked the same way to everyone produce far better predictions than conversation.
- **Reference checks are evidence.** Ask the referee what the person would struggle with, not whether they were good. The answer is almost always honest.
