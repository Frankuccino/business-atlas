Pipeline is the set of deals that might close, each valued and weighted by probability. It is how a sales organisation sees the future, and it is the earliest warning available.

The distinction from a forecast matters. A forecast is a promise; a pipeline is a picture. A pipeline tells you where the risk is — too few opportunities, or too many stuck at low probability — while there is still time to do something about it.

## Worked example

    Opportunity        Value    Probability   Weighted
    Acme               40,000       60%        24,000
    Beta                25,000       40%        10,000
    Gamma               60,000       80%        48,000
    Delta               15,000       10%         1,500
    Total              140,000                  83,500

Quota is $200,000 for the quarter. Weighted pipeline coverage is 83,500 / 200,000 = 0.4x, far below the 3x usually needed. The problem is visible: Delta is nearly dead weight and Beta is unlikely. Those two need attention now, not at quarter end.

## Watch for

- **Probabilities are opinions.** Salespeople optimise them. Require evidence for a high probability and audit the ones that never move.
- **Coverage is a ratio, not a total.** A large pipeline against a large quota can be as unhealthy as a small one against a small quota.
- **Stale pipeline is worse than no pipeline.** An opportunity that has not moved in two months should be removed. It inflates coverage while hiding the gap.
