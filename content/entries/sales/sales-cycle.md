Sales cycle is how long a deal takes to close. It is a structural property of your market — set by the buyer's process, the price, and the risk — not a performance metric you can simply improve.

Its importance is in the arithmetic it imposes. Revenue this quarter comes from pipeline created many months earlier, so the cycle length sets how far ahead you must be working.

## Worked example

A company closes $40,000 deals with a 90-day cycle. To close $400,000 in a quarter, it needs $400,000 of qualified pipeline created roughly 90 days earlier — about 10 deals in motion at all times.

Now the same company sells a $4,000 version with a 7-day cycle:

    400,000 / 4,000 = 100 deals
    But the pipeline only needs to exist 7 days ahead

The smaller deal needs far more volume but far less foresight, and it can be closed by a much less expensive seller. That is the core trade-off between a high-ACV and low-ACV motion.

## Watch for

- **Measure per segment.** A blended average across a self-serve and an enterprise motion describes neither.
- **The cycle has stages.** Knowing where time is spent — discovery, legal, procurement — tells you what to shorten.
- **Cycle length sets the payback of a hire.** A new salesperson on a 12-month cycle produces nothing for a year. Plan hiring ahead of revenue, not behind it.
