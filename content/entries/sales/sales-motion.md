A sales motion uses people to close deals. A self-serve motion uses the product and pricing page. They are not two versions of the same thing — they are different businesses with different economics.

The decision is arithmetic. A salesperson costs money whether or not they sell. If a customer is not worth more than that cost, a salesperson cannot serve them profitably.

## Worked example

    Salesperson fully loaded:   $180,000/year
    Expected quota:              $450,000/year
    Implied cost of sale:        40% of revenue

    Self-serve ACV:              $1,200/year
    Cost to serve via sales:     $480  →  the sale is unprofitable

    Enterprise ACV:              $60,000/year
    Cost to serve via sales:     $24,000 →  gross margin 60%, viable

The same sales team is ruinous at $1,200 and reasonable at $60,000. That is why low-ACV products must be self-serve, and why enterprise products almost always need a seller.

## Watch for

- **The motions blur.** Selling enterprise while leaving a self-serve page creates two conflicting price lists and confuses both buyers.
- **A sales motion hides product problems.** If the product cannot sell itself, a sales team will paper over the gap until the cost of sale becomes unsustainable.
- **Switching motions is expensive.** Moving up-market means retraining, re-pricing, and usually rebuilding the product. Decide early.
