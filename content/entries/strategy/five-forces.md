Five Forces asks whether an industry is structurally capable of producing profit. The five pressures are new entrants, supplier power, buyer power, substitutes, and rivalry.

The point is that a great company in a bad industry earns less than an average company in a good one. Structure dominates execution, which is why the framework is worth running before committing years to a market.

## Worked example

A specialty coffee roaster:

    New entrants        Low barrier — a roaster costs little to start. High pressure.
    Suppliers           Commodity beans, many sources. Low pressure.
    Buyers              Cafes and consumers, many choices. High pressure.
    Substitutes         Instant, tea, energy drinks. High pressure.
    Rivalry             Many roasters, undifferentiated. High pressure.

Four of five forces are adverse. The industry can contain good companies, but it will not produce structural margin, so profit depends on execution and branding rather than position. Compare a patent-protected drug: one force (new entrants) is blocked by regulation and the economics invert.

## Watch for

- **Define the industry tightly.** "Food" and "specialty coffee for offices" have completely different force profiles.
- **Forces change.** Buyer power rose sharply for many suppliers once procurement software made comparison trivial.
- **A favourable structure is not a strategy.** It tells you where to look, not what to build.
