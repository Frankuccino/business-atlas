A moat is a durable reason a customer stays with you even when a competitor offers the same thing cheaper. It is the economic term for defensibility.

The test is not whether you are better today. It is whether a well-funded competitor could replicate your offer in a year and take your customers. If they can, you have a lead, not a moat — and leads expire.

## Worked example

Four businesses, same product category, four different positions:

    A. Better UI            — copied in a quarter. No moat.
    B. Lower price          — undercut tomorrow. No moat.
    C. Data from 40,000 users that improves the product — copying the feature does not copy the data. Moat.
    D. Contracts running to 2028 with switching costs — a competitor must wait or pay to displace them. Moat.

A and B compete on effort, which anyone can match. C and D compete on accumulated position, which takes years to build and cannot be bought quickly.

## Watch for

- **A moat is not a feature.** Features are copied. Moats are network effects, switching costs, economies of scale, and regulation.
- **Moats can be temporary.** Technology changes destroy them. A retailer's location moat died with the internet.
- **Moats protect margin, not existence.** They let you keep profit; they do not make you immune to a market that stops wanting the product.
