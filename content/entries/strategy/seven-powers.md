7 Powers categorises advantage into seven mechanisms: scale economies, network economies, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, and process power.

Its usefulness is precision. "We have a moat" is unfalsifiable; "we have switching costs because migrating our data format takes six months" is a claim you can test and an investor can check.

## Worked example

    Scale economies     — unit cost falls as volume rises. Prerequisite: a large addressable market and a cost curve that actually bends.
    Network economies  — value rises with users. Prerequisite: connections between customers.
    Counter-positioning — a new model incumbents cannot copy without harming themselves. Prerequisite: an incumbent with a business model that blocks the response.
    Switching costs    — leaving is expensive. Prerequisite: integration into the customer's operations.
    Branding           — a price premium from trust. Prerequisite: repeated purchase and uncertainty about quality.
    Cornered resource  — exclusive access to something scarce. Prerequisite: a resource others cannot obtain.
    Process power      — embedded know-how that improves with repetition. Prerequisite: a long, stable production history.

A SaaS tool with deep integrations holds switching costs. A search engine holds scale economies and network economies. Neither holds branding in any meaningful sense, and claiming it would be a category error.

## Watch for

- **A power requires its prerequisite.** Claiming network effects for a product with no connections between users is a category error, not a strategy.
- **Powers have half-lives.** Counter-positioning works only while the incumbent is constrained.
- **Pick one to build.** Attempting all seven spreads effort and usually produces none.
