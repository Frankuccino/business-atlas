ARPU is revenue divided by users. ARPPU is revenue divided by *paying* users. The difference matters because the two move for different reasons.

ARPU rises when you sell more to existing customers or when the mix shifts toward bigger ones. ARPPU rises when each paying customer spends more. A falling ARPPU with a rising ARPU means you are acquiring many small customers, which usually means higher churn and lower lifetime value.

## Worked example

A company has 10,000 registered users and 2,000 paying ones. Monthly revenue is $60,000:

    ARPU  = 60,000 / 10,000 = $6.00
    ARPPU = 60,000 /  2,000 = $30.00

Now a price increase lifts revenue to $66,000 with the same user counts:

    ARPU  = $6.60   (+10%)
    ARPPU = $33.00  (+10%)

Both rose together, which is what a clean price increase looks like. If instead ARPU rose to $7.20 while ARPPU stayed at $30, the growth came from converting free users rather than from customers spending more — a different, and usually less durable, source.

## Watch for

- **Which denominator.** Reporting ARPU against paying users while comparing it to a competitor's ARPU against all users produces a meaningless comparison.
- **ARPU hides distribution.** A few very large customers can carry the average. Check the median.
- **See [ltv](#ltv).** ARPU is a period figure; LTV is the cumulative one. A rising ARPU with a falling LTV means customers are spending more but leaving sooner.
